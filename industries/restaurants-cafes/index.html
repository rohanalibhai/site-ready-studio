Where these sites usually fall short

Owners of restaurants, cafes, bakeries, food trucks and bars who want guests to find the menu, hours and a way to order or book in one tap.

The menu is a photo or a PDF

Guests pinch and zoom on a blurry image, and Google cannot read it. A menu as real text loads fast, reads well on a small screen and can be found by search.

Hours are out of date

A wrong closing time or a missing holiday schedule sends hungry people to a locked door. Hours need to be on the page, easy to change and the same everywhere you are listed.

Ordering and booking are hidden

If guests have to hunt for the reservation or pickup link, many give up. The next step should be the biggest button on the page, not a line of small print.

Questions from restaurants & cafes

Can guests order food through my site?

Yes, through a button that opens the ordering service you already use, such as your own online ordering page or a payment link in your account. I do not build a custom ordering system or take payments myself. That keeps your orders and your money in accounts that belong to you.

How do you handle a menu that changes often?

The menu is plain text, so a change means editing a line, not redoing an image. On the Care plan you text me the new items or prices and I update the page. Care Plus includes a seasonal menu refresh each month. You can also edit the files yourself, since they are yours.

Do I need a new site if I already have a Facebook page?

A social page is useful but it is not a menu, hours and map you control. People searching for a place to eat often want a quick answer without logging in to anything. A simple site gives them that, and it stays yours if platform rules change.
