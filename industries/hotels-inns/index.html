Where these sites usually fall short

Owners of independent hotels, inns, B&Bs and short-stay properties who want guests to see the rooms and ask to book direct.

1

Rooms are hard to compare

Guests want to see each room type, its bed size, who it suits and what it includes. A page for each room answers that without a long scroll.

2

Location and surroundings are vague

Travelers choose partly on what is nearby. A clear location page with directions, parking and local highlights helps them picture the stay.

3

The only way to book is a big platform

If the only visible option is a third-party site, you may pay fees on every booking. A direct-booking request path gives guests another choice.

Questions from hotels and inns

Can guests book and pay on my site?

The site sends a booking request to your email or links to the booking system you already use. I do not build a live booking engine or take payments myself. That keeps reservations, availability and card details in a system designed for them and under your control.

Can I show my rates?

Yes, if you supply them. Because rates change, many properties show a starting rate or say that rates vary by date, and ask guests to send a request. I never invent rates or show ratings I cannot source. You decide what appears.

Will this replace the big booking sites?

No, and it does not need to. Most properties keep listing on them. The site gives guests who find you elsewhere a place to learn more and request a booking direct. It also gives you a page you fully control, with your own photos and details.
