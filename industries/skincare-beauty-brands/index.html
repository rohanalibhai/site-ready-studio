Where these sites usually fall short

Owners of small skincare, cosmetics and body-care brands who sell products and need a clear, careful, trustworthy site.

1

Ingredients hidden in images

When the ingredient list is a picture of a label, shoppers cannot copy it, search it or check it for an allergy. Real text is easier to read and easier to trust.

2

Claims that go too far

Words like treats, cures or heals can create trouble for a cosmetic product. A careful site sticks to cosmetic benefits such as feel, appearance and routine.

3

No help choosing

A shopper who does not know which product fits their skin leaves without buying. A short guide by skin type or routine points them to a starting product.

Questions from skincare brands

Can the site say my product treats acne or eczema?

No. I write cosmetic claims only, such as how a product feels, how skin looks or what it is for in a routine. Words that suggest treating, curing or preventing a condition can cause problems, so I leave them off. I will flag anything risky and you approve every line.

Where do the ingredient lists come from?

From you. You send the lists as they appear on your labels, and I put them on each product page as real text. I do not invent ingredients or make up their benefits. If you have supplier notes or certifications, you tell me what you are happy to show.

Can I add reviews from customers?

Only real ones you have the right to share. I never write or invent reviews. If you have genuine comments, such as emails or messages you have permission to quote, I can lay them out cleanly. If you have none yet, the site simply leaves that section out.
