Where these sites usually fall short

Owners of clothing boutiques, vintage shops and fashion stores who want new arrivals, sizing and a clear path to buy or visit.

1

Old stock leads the page

Visitors see last season on the home page and assume the shop is stale. New arrivals should be the first thing they see, with older pieces a tap away.

2

No size or fit information

Shoppers leave when they cannot tell how something fits. A size guide and a note on fit remove the main reason people hesitate.

3

No clear path to buy or visit

If the site shows photos but no price, link or store details, a keen shopper has nowhere to go. Every item needs a next step: buy now, or come and see it.

Questions from clothing boutiques

Can I sell clothes directly on my site?

Yes, using buy buttons that go to a checkout in your own account, such as Shopify, Square or Etsy. I do not build a custom shopping cart. That keeps payments, inventory and customer details in tools you control and that already handle card security.

How many products can the site show?

The Catalog package includes up to 24 products with photos, prices and options. If you have more, extra items are $40 per 10. Many boutiques show a rotating selection of new arrivals and best sellers on the site, and send people to a fuller shop for the rest.

I mostly sell in the store. Do I still need this?

A site helps people decide to visit. It can show new arrivals, hours, parking and directions, and a size guide, with no checkout at all. In that case the Launch or Growth package may be enough, and I will tell you if Catalog is more than you need.
