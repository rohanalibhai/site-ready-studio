Where these sites usually fall short

Owners of local gift shops, bookstores, florists, hardware stores and specialty retailers who want shoppers to see what they stock and when to visit.

Nobody can tell what you carry

If your site shows only a logo and an address, a shopper cannot tell if you have what they want. A short list of categories and featured items answers that.

Store details are buried

Hours, parking and pickup options need to be near the top. Shoppers on a phone will not dig through menus to find them.

Facebook is the only place

A social page changes often and is hard to search. A simple site gives you one stable place that shows what you offer and how to reach you.

Questions from retail and gift shops

Do I have to sell online?

No. Many local shops use the site only to show what they carry, their hours and how to find them. If you later want online sales, I can add buy buttons that link to your own checkout, such as Square, Shopify or Etsy. Nothing about the site locks you in either way.

Can the site show if an item is in stock?

Not live. I do not build inventory software, so the site shows what you tell me is available. You can update it yourself, or text me changes on the Care plan. If you sell through Square or Shopify, their own pages can show stock for items linked from the site.

What about holiday hours and events?

A banner and a short events section handle both, and they are easy to change. On the Care plan, text me the new hours and I update the page. Care Plus includes a seasonal refresh each month, such as a holiday banner or a featured collection.
