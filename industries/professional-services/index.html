Where these sites usually fall short

Independent accountants, lawyers, consultants, real estate agents, insurance and financial professionals who need a credible site and a simple inquiry path.

1

It says what you do but not who for

A site that lists services without naming the clients you help feels generic. A clear statement of who you serve helps the right people see themselves in it.

2

Credentials are missing or vague

People look for licenses, qualifications and memberships before they inquire. If you have them, they should be stated plainly and exactly.

3

Getting in touch is awkward

A bare email address makes people hesitate. A short inquiry form that asks for the essentials makes the first step easy.

Questions from professional services

Can the site show my qualifications?

Yes, as you supply them. Give me the exact wording of your licenses, degrees or memberships, and I will show them in a clear section. I do not look them up or add any you did not give me. Where your profession has advertising rules, please confirm the wording with your own advisor.

Should the form collect client documents?

No. I recommend a simple form that asks only for the essentials, such as name, contact details and a short description. It sends an email and is not secure storage. Documents and sensitive details should go through a method you already trust, after the first conversation.

I only get clients by referral. Do I need a site?

Referrals often check you online before they call. A short, clear site confirms that you are real, shows what you do and gives them an easy way to reach you. A Launch site of a few pages is usually enough for that.
